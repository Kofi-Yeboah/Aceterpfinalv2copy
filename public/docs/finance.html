How money works at ACET

Nine flows, start to finish. Money arriving, budgets being set, every transaction being written down, bills being paid, the month being closed, and the reports that come out at the end. Each step says what happens, why it matters, where it is done, and what changes in the system when it does.

The one idea behind all of it

Finance never just records that money moved. It records where it came from and where it went, as a matched pair, every single time. That is why the module has so many screens: each one is a different place where that pair gets created, checked, or reported.

If you remember one thing, remember that nothing is ever deleted. A mistake is corrected by adding an opposite entry that cancels it, and both stay on the record. Finance is a history, not a current picture that gets edited.

A fund's life, from commitment to close

1
Finance

The fund is set up

Each pot of donor money gets its own record: a name, a short code, the donor it came from, and the project it belongs to if it is tied to one. Three numbers sit against it — the total committed, how much has gone out so far, and what is left.

Why it matters Donor money is not one big pool. Money given for rural health cannot quietly pay for something else. Keeping each fund separate is what makes that promise real rather than a policy on paper.
In the system

Finance › Funds Management

Each row is a fund. The balance column is the number people actually look at.

2
Finance

The fund carries a status

A fund is Available, Reserved, Depleted or Closed.

  • Available — there is money here and it can be committed.
  • Reserved — the money is spoken for, even though it has not left yet.
  • Depleted — spent out.
  • Closed — the grant is finished and the fund is wound up.
Why it matters Reserved is the status people forget and then regret. A balance that looks healthy but is already committed to three signed contracts is not money you can spend.
What changes when a fund is set up
  • Budgets can now be built against that money instead of against a hope.
  • The dashboard's funding and grant utilisation figures have something to count.
  • Anyone asking "can we afford this?" has a balance to point at.
Connects to

CRM › Grant Management — the agreement that created the fund. Finance › Project Budgets — where the money gets allocated. MEL › Dashboard — grant utilisation is this money being drawn down.

Revising is not editing. A revised budget keeps its earlier versions, with who changed it and why, so the number that was originally approved is still visible.

1
Department or project lead

There are two kinds of budget, and they stay separate

Operational budgets belong to a department — salaries, rent, software, travel — and run for a financial year. Project budgets belong to a project and carry the donor's name alongside the figures.

Why it matters They answer to different people. A department overspending is an internal management issue. A project overspending is a conversation with a donor. Mixing them into one list hides which of those you are looking at.

2
Finance

A budget request shows the department, the year, what is being asked for and what the current budget already is, so the approver sees the increase rather than just the total.

3
The system

The status works itself out

Every budget line reads On Track, Under Budget or Over Budget — and project budgets add At Risk. Nobody types these in. They come from comparing what has been spent to what was allocated.

Anything within five per cent either way counts as on target. Outside that, spending less than planned is favourable and spending more is unfavourable.

Why it matters Two things. First, a status nobody types can never disagree with the numbers next to it. Second, the five per cent band is what stops every tiny rounding difference being flagged as a problem — if everything is an exception, nobody looks at exceptions.
What changes once a budget is approved
  • It becomes the line that spending is measured against — before this, there is nothing to compare to.
  • Finance can answer the procurement plan question "is there money for this?" with a number.
  • Budget utilisation appears on the dashboard.
Connects to

Procurement › Approvals › Plan Approval — the finance station on a procurement plan checks the activity against this budget. Project Management — project budgets belong to projects. Funds Management — a project budget should not exceed the fund behind it.

Flow 03

Writing down what happened

The heart of the whole module. Every single thing that happens to money gets written down here, and it gets written down twice.

Written twice — what that actually means

Pay a supplier ₵5,000 and two things are true at once: the bank account is ₵5,000 lighter, and an expense of ₵5,000 has been incurred. Finance records both halves as one entry. The two halves must be equal — if they are not, something has been missed.

The entry form adds up both sides as you type and shows either Balanced or the exact amount you are out by. You cannot save an entry that does not balance. That is the single most important rule in the module, and it is not a warning you can click past.

Draftbeing written, freely editable
→
Submittedsent for checking
→
Approvedagreed as correct
→
Postednow part of the official record
→
Reversedcancelled — and it ends here

Going backwards is allowed, but only early. Submitted and Approved can be sent back to Draft. Once something is Posted the only way out is a reversal, and once Reversed nothing further can happen to it at all.

1
Accountant

The entry is written

A date, a description, a reference, and the lines — each naming an account and an amount on one side or the other. The entry also says what kind of entry it is, which matters at month end: a normal transaction, an adjustment, an accrual for something incurred but not yet invoiced, a currency revaluation, and so on.

In the system

Finance › Create Entries

Click Create Entry, fill the lines, then Save as Draft. The Save button stays greyed out until the two sides balance and every line has an account.

2
Accountant → Manager

Somebody else checks it

The person who writes an entry is not the person who approves it. The accountant submits; a manager or the finance director approves.

Why it matters This is the oldest control in accounting and the reason the four stages exist at all. One person alone should never be able to move money from writing it down to it being final.
In the system

Open the entry and use Move to Submitted, then Move to Approved. Each stage records who did it and when.

3
Finance

It is posted

Posting is the moment the entry stops being a proposal and becomes part of the books. Everything after that — the balances, the statements, the reports — includes it.

In the system

Move to Posted on a single entry, or select several on Finance › General Ledger and use Batch Posting to clear a month's worth at once.

What changes at this moment
  • Account balances move.
  • The trial balance, income statement and balance sheet all change.
  • The entry can no longer be edited — only reversed.
4
Finance

If it was wrong, it gets reversed — never deleted

Reversing does not touch the original. It creates a new entry that is the mirror image of it: everything that was on one side goes on the other, so the two cancel out to nothing. It is described as "Reversal of" the original and linked back to it.

The reversal arrives as a Draft, which means it has to be approved and posted like any other entry. Cancelling a mistake gets the same scrutiny as making the original entry did.

Why it matters The record shows what was thought to be true, and then shows it being corrected. An auditor can see both. If mistakes were simply deleted, the books would look perfect and prove nothing.
In the system

Open a posted entry and click Reverse. The button only appears on posted entries.

Entries that repeat

Rent, depreciation, insurance — the same entry every month with only the date changing. These are saved as templates with a frequency of monthly, quarterly or annually, and generated from the template rather than typed again each time. Fewer keystrokes, and no chance of the amount drifting because somebody mistyped it in March.

Connects to

Everything. Every other flow on this page finishes here — a payment, a depreciation charge, a currency revaluation, a month-end closing adjustment all become entries. This is where the whole system's money story is written down.

2
Finance

A supplier invoice is the strictest of them

An invoice against a contract passes four different people before finance pays it: the contract coordinator matches it to work that was actually accepted, procurement checks it against the contract, a supervisor gives final approval, and only then does finance pay.

Two things the system refuses outright: paying an invoice that would take the contract over its approved value, and paying for work that has not been accepted. Both send you back to fix the cause rather than letting the payment through.

In the system

Procurement › Invoices

Each invoice shows where it has got to and only the button for the stage it is actually at.

3
Finance

The payment is recorded as cash moving

Every receipt and payment is logged with a reference, a description, the method used, the amount, and the balance immediately after it. That last column is what makes the sequence checkable — you can see the position after each movement rather than just a list of amounts.

In the system

Finance › Cash Management

What changes when something is paid
  • The cash balance falls, and the running balance shows it.
  • The fund the money came from has more disbursed and less remaining.
  • The budget line it belongs to moves closer to its limit — and may flip to Over Budget.
  • For a contract invoice, the amount paid against that contract goes up, which affects what can still be invoiced.
Connects to

Procurement › Invoices and Contract Management — supplier payments start there. Payroll Management — the payroll run. Employee Self-Service — expenses, advances and petty cash. Funds Management — the pot the money came out of.

Flow 05

Checking the details still add up

The books keep one total for "money owed to us". Somewhere else is the list of every customer who owes it. Those two must agree.

Summary totalone line in the books
vs
Detailed listevery individual item
→
Agreenothing to do

If they do not agree, the gap is shown as an exact amount and the items that could not be matched are listed. It must be fixed before the month can be closed.

1
Finance

Five summary totals, five detailed lists

The books carry a single total for each of: money owed to us, money we owe, our fixed assets, our stock, and payroll. Behind each of those totals is a list of the individual items that make it up.

Why it matters Keeping the detail out of the main books stops them being unreadable. But it means there are now two places where the same fact is recorded, and two places can drift apart.
In the system

Finance › Control Accounts

2
Finance

The check is a subtraction

The summary total minus the sum of the detailed list. If the answer is zero, they agree. Anything else is a break, and it is shown as a number rather than a vague warning.

Every item on the detailed list records which part of the system created it, so an unmatched item can be traced back to the document behind it.

Why it matters This is why it is step 4 of the month-end close and not an afterthought. If the two do not agree and you close anyway, every report you produce afterwards is wrong in a way that is very hard to find later.
Connects to

Procurement — supplier invoices become the "money we owe" list. Asset Management — the asset register is the detail behind the fixed asset total. Payroll — the payroll list sits behind the payroll total.

Flow 06

Working in more than one currency

Donors give in dollars, euros and pounds. We spend in cedis. The books are kept in cedis, so everything else has to be converted — and the rate keeps moving.

1
Finance

There are three kinds of rate, and using the wrong one changes the answer

RateUsed forIn plain terms
SpotA single transactionThe rate on the day it happened
AverageIncome and spending over a periodThe typical rate across the month or quarter, because the money moved throughout it, not on one day
ClosingWhat we hold at period endThe rate on the last day, because that is what the balance is worth now

Rates are stored with the date they took effect, and a back-dated transaction is converted at the rate that applied then — not at today's rate.

In the system

Finance › Multi-Currency › FX Rates

2
Finance

At month end, foreign balances are restated

Say we hold $10,000. When it arrived the rate was 12, so the books show ₵120,000. At month end the rate is 13, so that same $10,000 is now worth ₵130,000. The books need to say ₵130,000, and the ₵10,000 difference has to be recorded somewhere.

That difference is called an unrealised gain — unrealised because nothing has actually been converted. We have not made ₵10,000, we simply hold something that is currently worth more. If the rate moves back next month, so does the gain.

Why it matters A donor grant sitting in dollars while costs are paid in cedis means the real value of that grant changes every month without anyone doing anything. Restating it is how the books stay honest about what the money is worth today.
In the system

Finance › Multi-Currency › Revaluation

This is step 3 of the month-end close.

3
Finance

The exposure view shows where the risk sits

Holdings grouped by currency, so it is visible how much of the organisation's money is exposed to a rate that could move against it.

In the system

Finance › Multi-Currency › Exposure Report

What changes after a revaluation
  • Foreign-currency balances are restated at the closing rate.
  • A gain or loss appears that nobody spent or earned — it came purely from the rate moving.
  • The balance sheet changes even though no money moved.

An asset's life in the books

1
Finance

The asset is registered

Name, category, what it cost, when it was bought, how long it is expected to last, and how its value will be written down.

In the system

Finance › Asset Management › Assets

Use Add New Asset. Categories cover property, IT equipment, vehicles, machinery and furniture.

2
Finance

Choosing how the value is written down

Four ways, and the choice should match how the thing actually loses value:

MethodIn plain termsSuits
Straight lineThe same amount every month for its whole lifeBuildings and furniture — things that wear out evenly. The usual choice.
Declining balanceA lot in the early years, less laterCars and laptops, which lose most of their value in the first year or two
Units of productionBased on how much it is used, not how long we have had itMachinery — a generator run constantly wears out faster than one barely used
Sum of years digitsAlso front-loaded, but on a set schedule rather than a percentageAssets with a predictable early decline
Why it matters The method decides how much cost lands in each year's accounts. Straight-lining a vehicle makes the early years look better than they are and leaves a nasty surprise when it is sold for far less than the books say it is worth.
In the system

Finance › Asset Management › Depreciation Mgmt

What changes when depreciation runs
  • Every asset's current value drops by that period's charge.
  • An expense appears in the accounts that nobody paid for in cash.
  • Assets reaching the end of their life flip to Fully Depreciated.

This is step 1 of the month-end close, which is why the asset register has to be up to date before a month can be closed. An asset bought but never registered is a cost that never gets charged.

Connects to

Procurement — assets arrive through a purchase order and a contract. Control accounts — the asset register is the detail behind the fixed asset total in the books.

Flow 08

Closing the month

Drawing a line under a period so the numbers stop moving and can be reported. It is a checklist, and the order is not optional.

Not openedon the calendar, not in use
→
Opennormal working month
→
Soft closechecklist under way — can still reopen
→
Hard closelocked, permanently

Soft close is the safety net. While a month is soft-closed it offers both Reopen and Continue Close. Once it is hard-closed there are no buttons at all — just a padlock. That difference is deliberate: a month under review can be pulled back, a month declared final cannot.

1
Finance

The twelve steps, in this order

  1. Run depreciation — charge this month's wear on every asset
  2. Accrue expenses — record things we have incurred but not been invoiced for yet
  3. Revalue foreign currency — restate foreign balances at the closing rate
  4. Reconcile the detailed lists — confirm they agree with the summary totals
  5. Generate closing entries — for the accounts that reset each year
  6. Post everything outstanding — nothing approved is left unposted
  7. Produce the trial balance — check the two sides of the books still agree
  8. Review final adjustments — last corrections before the numbers freeze
  9. Close income and spending — sweep them into the year's result
  10. Move the result to reserves — the profit or loss becomes part of accumulated funds
  11. Lock the period — nothing more can be posted
  12. Produce the statements — the reports for the closed month
In the system

Finance › Period Management

Click Start Close on an open month. A progress bar shows how far through you are, and a step cannot be ticked while an earlier one is still outstanding.

2
—

Why that order and no other

Each step depends on the one before it. Depreciation and accruals have to be in before anything is revalued, or you are revaluing an incomplete picture. Revaluation has to be done before the detailed lists are reconciled, or they will not match. Everything has to be posted before a trial balance means anything. And the month is only locked once the numbers it is about to report have stopped moving.

Why it matters Skipping a step does not just miss that step. It quietly invalidates every step after it, and the resulting statements look completely normal.
What changes when a month is hard-closed
  • Nothing further can be posted into it — a late invoice has to go into the next month instead.
  • The statements for that month become final.
  • The decision cannot be undone.

Why the audit trail is worth more than it looks It records the value before and after every change, not just that something changed. "Someone edited this" is not evidence. "This was 5,000, and Ama changed it to 8,000 on 14 March" is.

The dashboard

The finance dashboard collects the numbers people ask for most — income against spending, cash flow, budget utilisation, where funding comes from, who owes us and how long they have owed it, who we owe, payroll by department, bank balances and anything waiting for a decision. Panels can be switched off, so each person can keep the ones they use.

Money owed is grouped by how overdue it is — current, one to two months, two to three, three to four, and beyond four. The older a debt gets, the less likely it is ever to be paid, which is why age is shown rather than just a total.

Across the system

How finance connects to everything else

Finance is the module everything else eventually arrives at. These are the doors between it and the rest of the system.

FromWhat crossesWhat finance does with it
Procurement A purchase requisition that has passed its department head and procurement Finance is the third and final check. It confirms the money exists, and its decision moves the requisition on for everyone else.
Procurement A procurement plan awaiting budget verification Finance checks the activity against the budget line before it can become a real purchase.
Procurement A supplier invoice that has passed three checks Finance pays it, which reduces the fund, moves the budget line, and increases what has been paid against that contract.
Procurement A supplier's bank details Finance verifies them. Until they are verified, that supplier cannot be paid.
Payroll The monthly payroll run Finance approves it before staff are paid, and the cost appears by department.
Employee Self-Service Expense claims, travel advances, petty cash Finance approves and pays them.
Project Management Projects and their budgets, and time booked to them Finance tracks spend by project and reports budget against actual.
CRM Grant agreements with donors Finance turns them into funds with balances that can be spent against.
Monitoring & Evaluation Grant utilisation and funding by donor Finance supplies the money side of the picture that MEL reports alongside results.
The pattern worth noticing

Finance is almost never the first step and almost always the last. Something is requested somewhere else, checked by the people who understand it, and only then reaches finance — whose job is to confirm the money exists, release it, and write down what happened. That is why finance approvals sit at the end of every chain rather than the beginning.

Reference

Words people use

The terms that come up in every finance conversation, in plain language.

Entry
One recorded event — money moving, or a value changing. Always written with two matching halves.
Posting
Making an entry final and part of the official books. Before posting it is a proposal; after, it counts.
Reversal
Cancelling a posted entry by adding its exact opposite. The original stays visible.
Ledger
The complete record of everything posted.
Account
A named bucket things are recorded against — a bank account, a category of spending, an asset.
Chart of accounts
The full list of those buckets, and how they are organised.
Accrual
Recording something in the month it actually happened, even though the invoice has not arrived yet.
Depreciation
Spreading the cost of a long-lived item across the years it will be used.
Period
A month. Periods are opened, worked in, then closed.
Soft close / hard close
Soft close means the checklist is under way and the month can still be reopened. Hard close means locked for good.
Trial balance
A list of every account's total, used to check the books still balance before producing anything else.
Variance
The gap between what was budgeted and what was spent. Under five per cent is treated as on target.
Unrealised gain or loss
A change in what foreign money is worth because the exchange rate moved. Nothing was bought or sold — it is on paper until it is converted.
Control account
A single summary total in the books that stands for a long detailed list kept elsewhere.
Reconciliation
Confirming that summary total and that detailed list still agree.
Fund
A pot of donor money with its own balance, kept separate because it was given for a purpose.
Ageing
Grouping money owed by how long it has been outstanding. Old debts are less likely to be collected.
Audit trail
The permanent record of who changed what, and what it was before and after.
