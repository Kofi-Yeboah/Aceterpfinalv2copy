01

How the module is built

Finance is the largest module in the system — sixteen sidebar entries, twenty-six routes, and a shared domain layer underneath. That domain layer is what distinguishes it from a set of unrelated screens.

The shared domain layer

Four files under components/finance/ hold everything the screens agree on:

FileWhat it holds
types.ts21 exported types and interfaces — the account, the journal entry and its lines, fiscal years and periods, FX rates, entities, intercompany transactions, audit log entries, roles and permissions, sub-ledger entries, budget versions.
constants.tsThe controlled vocabularies: 8 ledger types, 10 journal entry types, 5 journal statuses, 4 period states, the 12-step closing template, 8 RBAC roles and the full permission matrix, the 6 account segments and their code lists, 6 currencies.
helpers.tsThe calculations: double-entry validation, FX conversion and rate lookup, currency formatting, variance, unrealised gain/loss, ageing buckets, entry numbering, and the status-transition map that defines which state may follow which.
mockData.tsTwelve seeded datasets — entities, currencies, FX rates, accounts, journal entries, fiscal years, recurring templates, intercompany transactions, the audit log, user roles, sub-ledger entries and revaluation entries.

How state actually works

This matters more than anything else on this page, so it is stated plainly rather than left to be discovered. Each finance screen holds its own copy of the seed data in React component state (useState(mockJournalEntries) and similar). Two consequences follow, and neither is a bug so much as a boundary:

Read this before demonstrating

Changes do not travel between screens. A journal entry created on Create Entries will not appear on General Ledger — the two screens each loaded their own copy of the same seed array.

Changes do not survive navigation. Leaving a screen and coming back re-mounts it from the seed data, so posted entries, closed periods and approved accounts revert.

Within a single screen, everything works: create an entry, submit it, approve it, post it, reverse it, and each transition behaves correctly and updates the totals. That is the right scope to demonstrate in.

The procurement module solved this with module-level stores under lib/ that publish changes to every subscribed screen. Finance has no equivalent — with one exception, covered in section 15.

What an account records

Account governance

An account is not usable the moment it is typed in. It moves through a lifecycle:

Draft→Pending Approval→Approved→Active→Inactive

The governance modal captures the reason for each change and appends it to the version history, so the chart of accounts carries its own audit trail independent of the transaction log. Deactivating rather than deleting is the only route out — an account with postings behind it can never be removed.

03

Journal entries

Create Entries

The entry form is where the module's core control lives. An entry has a header — type, ledger, date, description, reference, currency, fiscal period — and two or more lines, each naming an account, a description, and a debit or a credit.

The double-entry gate

validateDoubleEntry() totals the debits and the credits and compares them. The Save button is disabled unless three things are all true:

  • Debits equal credits, within a hundredth of a unit — Math.abs(totalDebit - totalCredit) < 0.01
  • The total is greater than zero, so an all-zero entry cannot be saved as "balanced"
  • Every line names an account

A live indicator on the form reads Balanced in green or shows the exact difference in red as you type. The tolerance exists because floating-point arithmetic on decimal currency will not otherwise reach exact equality.

Entry types

Ten types, each with a defined purpose:

TypePurpose
StandardRegular business transactions
AdjustingPeriod-end adjustments for accruals and deferrals
ReversingAuto-reversal of a prior period accrual
RecurringGenerated from a template on a schedule
AccrualRecognition of earned revenue or incurred expense
ClosingPeriod-end closing of temporary accounts
OpeningNew period opening balances
RevaluationForeign currency revaluation adjustment
EliminationIntercompany elimination
ReclassificationMoving a balance between accounts

Ledger types

Eight ledgers keep different accounting treatments apart on the same underlying transactions: Primary (day-to-day), Secondary (an alternative GAAP), Statistical (non-monetary quantities), Budget, Consolidation, Tax, Reporting (management and regulatory adjustments) and Memo (informational, no financial impact).

The status lifecycle

Draft→Submitted→Approved→Posted→Reversed

getStatusTransitions() is the single definition of what may follow what, and the screens read from it rather than each deciding for themselves. Submitted and Approved can both fall back to Draft. Posted can only go to Reversed. Reversed is terminal — nothing follows it. Each transition stamps who did it and when: submittedBy, approvedBy, postedBy, reversedBy, each with its date.

How a reversal is built

Reversing a posted entry does not edit it. The original stays posted and untouched; a new entry is generated with every line's debit and credit swapped, typed as Reversing, described as "Reversal of {entry no}", linked back through reversalOf, and created in Draft — so the reversal itself has to be approved and posted like anything else. The approver and poster of the original are deliberately cleared on the copy.

Recurring templates

A template holds a name, an entry type, a set of lines and a frequency — Monthly, Quarterly or Annually — plus the next due date, the date it last generated, and an active flag. Rent, depreciation and amortisation are the usual candidates: the lines are fixed, only the period changes.

04

General ledger

General Ledger

The ledger view is the same entry population seen from the posting side rather than the authoring side. It filters by status and searches by entry number or description, and each row expands into its lines with the account code, account name, debit, credit and the running totals.

Batch posting

Rows carry checkboxes and a select-all. The batch modal takes everything selected and posts it in one action, which is how a month's approved entries are cleared rather than one at a time. The selection is intentionally limited to entries eligible for the transition — posting is not offered for something still in Draft.

05

Period management and close

Period Management

A fiscal year holds twelve periods plus any adjusting periods. Each period carries a state, and the state decides whether anything may still be posted into it.

StateMeaningWhat may follow
Not OpenedThe period exists on the calendar but is not yet accepting entriesOpen
OpenNormal postingSoft Close
Soft CloseThe close checklist is under way; the period can still be reopenedHard Close, or back to Open
Hard CloseLocked. Nothing further posts to this periodNothing — terminal
The one-way door

Soft Close is reversible and offers both Reopen and Continue Close. Hard Close offers nothing at all — the row renders a padlock and the word Closed. That asymmetry is the control: a period under review can be pulled back, a period declared final cannot.

The twelve-step close

Reaching Hard Close means working a checklist, and the steps must be completed in order — the handler refuses to mark step n complete while any earlier step is outstanding. A progress bar shows how far through the period is, and the period cannot be finalised until every step is done.

  1. Run depreciation across all fixed assets
  2. Accrue expenses not yet invoiced
  3. Revalue foreign currency at closing rates
  4. Reconcile sub-ledgers against GL control accounts
  5. Generate closing entries for temporary accounts
  6. Post all pending approved entries to the GL
  7. Generate the trial balance and verify debits equal credits
  8. Review and approve final adjusting entries
  9. Close revenue and expense to income summary
  10. Transfer net income or loss to retained earnings
  11. Lock the period
  12. Generate financial statements for the closed period

The ordering is not decorative. Depreciation and accruals must be in before revaluation; revaluation before reconciliation; everything posted before the trial balance means anything; and the period is only locked once the numbers it will report have stopped moving.

06

Multi-currency

Multi-Currency

Six currencies are configured — US Dollar, Ghanaian Cedi, Euro, British Pound, Nigerian Naira and Kenyan Shilling — with the Cedi as the functional currency. The screen has three tabs.

FX rates

Every rate carries a type, and the type decides what it may be used for:

Rate typeUsed for
SpotTranslating an individual transaction on its own date
AveragePeriod figures — revenue and expense translated at the period average
ClosingBalance sheet items at period end, and the basis for revaluation

Rates are looked up by currency pair, type and date, taking the most recent rate effective on or before the date asked for, so a back-dated entry is translated at the rate that applied then rather than today's.

Revaluation

A monetary balance held in a foreign currency was recorded at one rate and is worth something different at period end. Revaluation measures that difference:

unrealised gain/loss = (original amount × closing rate) − (original amount × original rate)

Each revaluation entry keeps the account, the currency, the original amount and rate, the revalued amount and closing rate, and the resulting gain or loss, tied to the period it belongs to. It is unrealised because nothing has been converted — the position is simply being restated. This is step 3 of the close.

Exposure report

The exposure tab aggregates holdings by currency to show where the organisation is carrying FX risk — useful when donor funds arrive in one currency and are spent in another.

07

Control accounts and reconciliation

Control Accounts

A control account is the single GL line that stands for a whole sub-ledger. Five types are supported: AR (receivables), AP (payables), FA (fixed assets), Inventory and Payroll. The detail — every customer, every supplier, every asset — lives in the sub-ledger; the GL carries only the total.

What reconciliation checks

The test is arithmetic and unforgiving:

difference = control account balance − sum of sub-ledger entries reconciled when |difference| < 0.01

Anything else is a break, shown as an amount rather than a warning symbol. Sub-ledger entries each carry a sourceModule and sourceDocumentId, so an unmatched item can be traced back to the document that created it, and a matched flag that drives the unmatched-items panel.

Why this is step 4 of the close

If the sub-ledgers do not agree with the GL, every statement produced after that point is wrong in a way that is very hard to find later. The reconciliation screen states it directly: unmatched entries must be resolved before period close.

08

Entities and intercompany

Three entities are configured — ACET Ghana, ACET Nigeria and ACET Kenya — each with its own functional currency and country. The entity is the first segment of every account code, so entity separation is built into the chart rather than bolted on.

An intercompany transaction records a movement from one entity to another: the date, both entities, both accounts, the amount and currency, and a status of Pending, Posted or Eliminated. Elimination is the point — money moved between two entities of the same organisation is not income to the group, so on consolidation those transactions are eliminated, and the elimination entry is linked back to the transaction it cancels.

09

Budgeting and planning

Budgeting & Planning

Budgets are split the same way procurement plans are, and for the same reason — different money, different owners.

ScreenHoldsTracked by
Operational BudgetsDepartmental budgets by category and fiscal year — salaries, software, rent, campaigns, professional services, training, travel, hardwareAllocated, spent, remaining, percentage used
Project BudgetsBudgets belonging to a project, carrying the donor and the project alongside the figuresThe same, plus an At Risk state that operational budgets do not have

Status is derived, not typed

A budget line reads On Track, Under Budget or Over Budget (project budgets add At Risk) from its own utilisation percentage. Nobody sets it by hand, so it cannot disagree with the numbers beside it. The general variance helper applies the same logic wherever budget meets actual:

variance = budgeted − actual percentage = variance ÷ budgeted × 100 status = |percentage| ≤ 5% → On Target variance > 0 → Favorable otherwise → Unfavorable

The five-percent band is what stops every ordinary rounding difference reading as a variance worth investigating.

Budget versions are modelled — version number, who changed it, the status through Draft, Submitted, Approved and Revised, a description of the change, and the approver with their date — so a revised budget keeps its history rather than overwriting it.

10

Funds and cash

Funds management

A fund is donor money with a name, a code, the donor, optionally a project, and three figures: total, disbursed and balance. Its status — Available, Reserved, Depleted or Closed — is what tells a budget holder whether the money behind a commitment actually exists. This is the screen that makes restricted funding visible: money from a donor for a purpose is not general reserves.

Cash management

A running cash record: each transaction is a Receipt or a Payment, with a reference number, description, category, amount, payment method, and the balance after it. The balance column makes the sequence auditable — every row shows what the position was immediately after that movement.

11

Assets and depreciation

Asset Management

The asset register holds the asset ID, name, category — Real Estate, IT Equipment, Vehicles, Machinery, Furniture — the acquisition date and cost, the current value, the depreciation method, the useful life, and a status of Active, Fully Depreciated or Disposed.

Four depreciation methods

MethodShape of the chargeSuits
Straight LineThe same amount every period across the useful lifeBuildings, furniture — anything consumed evenly. The default here.
Declining BalanceA fixed percentage of the reducing carrying value, so heavy early and light laterVehicles and IT equipment, which lose most value early
Units of ProductionCharged per unit of output rather than per periodMachinery whose wear tracks usage, not time
Sum of Years DigitsAccelerated, but on a fixed declining schedule rather than a percentageAssets with a predictable but front-loaded decline

Running depreciation is step 1 of the period close, which is why the asset register has to be current before a period can be closed — an asset acquired but not registered is depreciation that never gets charged.

The one that is fully wired

Finance › Approvals › Procurement Requests is genuinely connected. It reads live requisitions from the procurement store, is gated on the pr.approveFinance capability, computes days in stage, flags variance against the approved plan, and its decision moves the requisition forward for every other screen in the system. Approving there is a real state change; approving elsewhere in this menu is not yet.

13

Reporting and analytics

Reporting & Analytics

Financial statements

Four statements, each computed from the same trial balance rather than entered separately:

StatementWhat it shows
Trial BalanceEvery account with its debit and credit total for the period, and the proof that the two sides agree
Income StatementRevenue less expenses, giving net income for the period
Balance SheetAssets, liabilities and equity as at period end
Closing BalancesPre-closing balance, the closing entry, and the post-closing balance per account

The closing-balance view is the one worth understanding, because it shows the mechanics rather than the result: net income is computed as revenue minus expenses and then transferred as a single closing entry to retained earnings (account 3100). Every other account's post-closing balance is simply its debit less its credit. That is step 10 of the close made visible.

The other four report areas

ReportContents
Project ReportsFinancial performance by project, including budget against actual
Audit TrayItems an auditor has queried or flagged, held for response
Audit TrailThe immutable log — timestamp, user, action, module, entity, before and after values, IP address
Timesheet ReportsTime booked against projects, the bridge between payroll cost and project cost

The audit log records nine action types — Create, Update, Delete, Approve, Reject, Post, Reverse, Close and Open — and keeps the before and after values rather than just noting that something changed. That distinction is what makes it usable as evidence.

Finance dashboard

Sixteen configurable panels, each of which can be shown or hidden: two rows of KPIs, revenue against expenses, cash flow and balance trend, budget utilisation, expense breakdown, funding sources, AR ageing, AP ageing, payroll by department, bank and fund balances, GL activity, payment voucher status, pending finance actions, alerts, and recent GL transactions. Ageing uses fixed buckets — Current, 31–60, 61–90, 91–120, and 120+ days.

Roles and permissions

Eight finance roles are defined, each with an explicit permission matrix across ten modules and seven actions — Create, Read, Update, Delete, Approve, Post and Reverse.

RoleShape of access
Finance DirectorThe only role with Delete and Reverse across the ledger, and the only one besides the Auditor who can see Access Control
Finance ManagerEverything the Director has except Delete, Reverse and Access Control
AccountantCreates, updates and posts entries, but cannot approve them
AP ClerkCreates journal entries; read-only everywhere else
AR ClerkThe same rights as the AP Clerk, on the receivable side
Budget AnalystFull create and update on budgeting; read-only on everything else
Financial AnalystRead-only everywhere
AuditorRead-only everywhere, plus read access to Access Control
The separation that matters

The Accountant may post but not approve; the Finance Manager and Director may approve. That split is the whole point of the four-status entry lifecycle — the person who writes the entry is not the person who authorises it.

Declared, not yet enforced

This matrix is a complete, coherent specification and the Access Control screen presents it. It is not currently wired to the finance screens — the buttons on those screens do not consult it before acting. Procurement's equivalent (lib/currentUser.ts) does gate its controls this way, and is the working model for connecting this up.

16

What is real, what is presentation

Stated plainly so that nobody demonstrates something that will not hold up, and so the build order is obvious.

AreaStatus
Domain modelReal. 21 types, complete and internally consistent. This is the module's strongest asset.
Double-entry validationReal. Computed, enforced on the Save button, shown live as you type.
Status transitionsReal. One shared definition, applied consistently; Reversed and Hard Close are genuinely terminal.
Reversal mechanicsReal. Generates a correctly mirrored draft linked to the original.
Close checklist orderingReal. Steps cannot be completed out of sequence.
Sub-ledger reconciliationReal. Difference computed against a 0.01 tolerance, unmatched items surfaced.
FX and revaluation mathsReal. Rate lookup respects type and date; gain/loss correctly computed.
Variance and ageingReal. Derived from the figures, never typed.
Statement derivationReal. Net income and closing balances computed from the trial balance.
Procurement Requests approvalReal. Store-backed, capability-gated, and its decision propagates.
Persistence between screensNot yet. Each screen holds its own copy of the seed data.
RBAC enforcementNot yet. Fully specified, presented on screen, not consulted by the controls.
Approval queues other than Procurement RequestsNot yet. Local state; decisions do not travel.
Cross-module figuresNot yet. Dashboard, budgets, funds and cash are seeded rather than aggregated from source.
If this is built out, the order that pays

A store first. A lib/financeStore.ts holding accounts, entries, periods and rates with the same subscribe-and-notify pattern the procurement stores use would make every existing screen agree with every other, without changing any of the logic already written. Everything else — enforcing the permission matrix, feeding the dashboard from real figures, connecting the remaining approval queues — becomes straightforward once state is shared, and stays awkward until it is.
