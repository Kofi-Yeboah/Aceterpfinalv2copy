How ACET buys things

The whole process, start to finish, in ten plain steps — and exactly where each one happens in the system.

1 · Suppliers join 2 · We plan 3 · Someone asks 4 · Bosses say yes 5 · We shop around 6 · Offers come in 7 · We pick & sign 8 · They deliver 9 · We pay 10 · We score them
1
Supplier Management

A supplier joins our list

A company or a consultant tells us they want to work with ACET. They send their papers — business certificate, tax certificate, bank details, ID. We check the papers are real, that none have expired, and that the name isn't on any banned list. If everything is fine, they get an ID number and go on our approved list.

Why it matters: we can only buy from people on this list. It stops us handing money to a stranger.

In the system

Procurement › Supplier Management › Onboard

Click Onboard, choose Firm or Individual, fill the form, and attach each document with its expiry date. Finish with Complete Onboarding.

As you type the name, the screen warns you straight away if it looks like a duplicate or matches a banned list.

Then it has to be approved: Procurement › Approvals › Supplier Registrations The new supplier waits there until Procurement clicks Approve, and the button refuses while mandatory documents are missing.

2
Procurement Plan

We write down what we plan to buy

At the start of the year, each team lists what they will need, roughly what it costs, and when. Procurement checks it follows the rules. Finance checks there is money for it. Only when both agree does it go on the plan.

Why it matters: if it isn't on the plan, it can't be bought. No surprise spending.

In the system

Procurement › Purchase Plan › Departmental Plans

Pick your department's tab and click Add activity. Fill in the activity, the cost and the dates. When you type the cost, the system suggests the buying method for you and shows the value bands. Then Save & submit for review — or Save as draft to finish later. Work paid for by a project goes under Project Plans instead, one plan per project.

Procurement › Approvals › Departmental Plan Approval

Two queues here: Procurement review and Finance verification. Open an item, click Review, then either approve it or Reject with reason. It only becomes usable after both queues have passed it. Project plans have their own queue, Project Plan Approval, because they are checked against the project's budget rather than a department's.

3
Purchase Requisition

Someone asks to buy something

When the time comes, the person who needs the item fills in a request form. They pick the item from the plan, say what it will cost, say which fund pays for it, and attach a description of exactly what they want.

Why it matters: the system won't let them send it until every part is filled in properly. It tells them exactly what's missing.

In the system

Procurement › Purchase Requisitions › Add New Requisition

Pick the plan item from the dropdown, fill the form, attach the Terms of Reference (for services) or Technical Specifications (for goods), then Submit Requisition.

If anything is missing, the button won't go through — red messages appear on the exact fields that need fixing.

4
Approvals

The bosses say yes

The request goes up the ladder: first their manager, then Procurement and Finance together (both at the same time, to save days), and if it costs more than $10,000, the COO as well. Anyone can say no — but they must write down why, and then the person fixes it and sends it again.

Why it matters: several people check before any money is committed, and nothing gets rejected without a reason.

In the system

Procurement › Approvals › Purchase Requisitions

Procurement › Approvals › Senior Management Approval

Each approver opens their own queue, reads the request, then clicks Approve or Reject. Reject won't submit until a reason is typed in.

If it was rejected, the requester opens it and clicks Resubmit for Approval after fixing it. They can also click Withdraw to cancel a request that's no longer needed.

5
Sourcing

We look for someone to supply it

Now Procurement goes shopping. How they shop depends on the size. Something small: ask a few suppliers for prices. Something medium: invite a short list. Something big: advertise it openly so anyone can bid.

Why it matters: the bigger the spend, the more competition we're required to run — that's how we get a fair price.

In the system

Procurement › Sourcing

You don't create anything here — the case appears on its own as soon as the requisition is approved. Open it and you'll see a numbered stepper with the exact stages for that buying method.

Procurement can still click Change Method; if the new choice doesn't match the value band, the system asks for a written reason first.

6
Sourcing

Suppliers send in their offers

Suppliers receive the tender documents and send their offers back. Each one is logged against the tender in a sealed envelope. Nobody at ACET can open it until the closing date has passed. The technical part is opened first and the prices later, so quality is judged before anyone sees the money.

Why it matters: nobody can peek early, and nobody can change their offer after the deadline.

In the system

Our side — in the sourcing case, upload the tender documents, then click Publish on the Advertisement step (open tenders) or pick the invited firms on the Invitation step (restricted tenders).

As offers arrive — on the Bid Opening step click Record Bid Received and log the supplier, the date and the reference. Each one gets a receipt number and a timestamp, and stays sealed.

Opening day — back in the sourcing case: Close Tender, then Open Technical Envelope, and later Open Financial Envelope. The system refuses to open anything early.

7
Award & Contract

We pick a winner and sign

The offers are scored and a winner is chosen. We check one last time that the winner is still allowed to work with us. Then the request number becomes the contract number, and we write down the deliverables, the dates and the payment schedule.

Why it matters: the same number follows the job from start to finish, so any payment can always be traced back to the original request.

In the system

In the sourcing case, go to the last step and click Award Contract. Pick the winner from the list — blocked suppliers can't be picked, and flagged ones need management sign-off first.

The contract is created automatically. For goods you can also click Generate Purchase Order, which routes to the COO for signature if it's $10,000 or more.

8
Deliverables

They do the work, and we check it

The supplier delivers. The contract coordinator uploads the proof — a report, a delivery note, an inspection form. Then somebody else has to look at it and accept it.

Why it matters: the person who uploads the proof cannot approve their own work. Two different people, always.

In the system

Procurement › Contract Management › open the contract › Deliverables

The coordinator clicks Upload Deliverable. A second person then clicks Accept or Reject. Full detail is in the contract flow.

9
Invoice & Payment

They send a bill, and we pay it

The supplier sends an invoice. It has to be matched to work we have already accepted. Then Procurement checks it against the contract, a supervisor approves it, and only then does Finance pay — recording the date, the amount and the bank reference.

Why it matters: we never pay for something we haven't received and checked. If anything looks wrong, anyone in the chain can send the bill back with a reason.

In the system

Procurement › Invoices

This screen shows every bill across every contract, grouped by which stage it's waiting at. Open one and the only button you'll see is the one for the stage it's actually at. Full detail is in the contract flow.

10
Close-out & Rating

We close the job and give them a score

When everything is delivered and paid, we check the list is complete, print a completion certificate, and score the supplier out of 10 — on quality, on being on time, on cost, on following the rules, and on how they behaved.

Why it matters: a score below 5 puts a flag on them. Next time, they need special permission before we can use them again.

In the system

Procurement › Contract Management › open the contract › Performance, then Close-Out

Score the supplier on the Performance tab, then finish on Close-Out with Close Contract. The score lands on the supplier's profile in Supplier Management by itself.

And then it starts again

The score from step 10 decides who is allowed back at step 1. Good suppliers get more work. Poor ones don't — unless management specifically approves it.

Demonstrating this to a room? Use the name badge at the top-right of the screen to switch between Felix (Procurement), Abena (Finance), James (Department Head) and Nana (COO). The system deliberately won't let one person approve their own work, so you need to switch to walk a request all the way through.

The words people use

Supplier
A company or person we buy from.
Procurement plan
The shopping list for the year.
Requisition
The form someone fills in to ask to buy something.
Sourcing
Looking for someone to supply it.
Tender / bid
An advert asking suppliers to offer a price, and the offer they send back.
Award
Choosing the winner.
Deliverable
A thing the supplier promised to hand over.
Invoice
The bill.
Variation
A change to a signed contract — more money, more time, or different work.
Close-out
Finishing the job properly and filing everything.
