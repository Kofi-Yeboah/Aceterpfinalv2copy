How ACET finds a supplier

What happens between "the request is approved" and "we have a signed contract" — in eight plain steps.

1 · The case appears 2 · Choose how to shop 3 · Prepare the papers 4 · Tell the suppliers 5 · Sealed offers arrive 6 · Open the envelopes 7 · Score and negotiate 8 · Award and sign

First — the four ways we can shop

The bigger the money, the more competition we're required to run. The system suggests the right one for you based on the value.

Direct Selection

up to $5,000

Go straight to one supplier. Always needs a written reason explaining why only they can do it.

Request for Quotation

$5,000 – $50,000

Ask a handful of known suppliers for prices and compare them. No public advert.

Limited Competition

$50,000 – $200,000

Invite a shortlist to bid. Needs a reason for why the market was restricted.

Open Competition

above $200,000

Advertise publicly. Anyone qualified can bid.

1
Initiation

The case appears by itself

Nobody creates anything here. The moment a purchase request finishes its approvals, a sourcing case appears in the list on its own, carrying everything across — what's needed, the budget, who asked for it, and which fund pays.

Why it matters: nothing can be sourced that didn't come through an approved request. There's no way to start buying something halfway down the process.

In the system

Procurement › Sourcing

The new case is in the list with its own number, e.g. SRC-2026-004, linked back to the request that created it. Open it to see a numbered stepper down the left.

The requester is attached to the case automatically, so they get told at every stage without having to chase anyone.

2
Method

We confirm how we're going to shop

The buying method already came across from the plan and the value. Procurement can still change it — but if the new choice doesn't match the value band, the system asks for a written reason before it will accept it.

Why it matters: people can override the rule when there's a genuine reason, but never quietly. The reason is kept on the record.

In the system

In the open case, click Change Method. The dropdown shows all four methods with their value bands, and warns you if your choice sits outside the band for this amount.

Confirm with Confirm Method Change. The steps down the left rebuild themselves for the new method — anything you'd already finished is kept.

3
Solicitation

We prepare the papers

Before we can ask anyone for a price, we write down exactly what we want: the specification for goods, or the terms of reference for a service. Plus how we'll score the offers — decided before we see any of them.

Why it matters: agreeing how you'll mark the answers before the answers arrive is what stops the scoring being bent to fit a favourite.

In the system

The first step is called Solicitation (ITB) for goods or Solicitation (RFP) for services. Click Upload Document against each required slot.

Every upload keeps its history — replacing a file with Upload New Version keeps the old one visible rather than overwriting it.

4
Advertise or Invite

We tell the suppliers

For an open tender, we publish it and record wherever it was advertised. For a limited one, we pick a shortlist and only they can see it. For a direct selection, we pick the single supplier.

Why it matters: whoever we pick here is checked against the approved supplier list at that exact moment. Blocked suppliers simply can't be selected.

In the system

Open tender — the Advertisement step. Tick where it's being advertised and click Publish Now. The advert and its channels are recorded against the case.

Limited or RFQ — the Invite Bidders step. Pick from the supplier list, say whether you want a technical proposal, a financial one, or both, then Send Invitations.

Direct — the step is called Select Supplier and lets you pick only one.

Not on our list yet? Register Supplier adds them without leaving the screen.

Who you can and can't pick
  • Blocked suppliers can't be selected at all — the reason is shown next to their name.
  • Flagged suppliers, or anyone scoring under 5, need a management sign-off and a written justification before you can invite them.
  • Everyone else is listed with their rating, sorted best first.
5
Submissions

Sealed offers arrive

Suppliers send their offers in, and each is logged against the tender. Each one is sealed the moment it is recorded and given a receipt number. We can see that an offer arrived and exactly when — but not what's inside it.

Why it matters: nobody at ACET can read an offer early, and no supplier can change theirs after the deadline. Both of those are enforced by the system, not by trust.

In the system

On the Bid Opening step, log each offer with Record Bid Received — supplier, date and reference. Each gets a submission ID and stays sealed until the official opening.

Our side — the Online Submissions step lists who has bid and when, each marked Sealed. Bids that came in by hand can be added with Record Bid Received, and both kinds sit in one list tagged by where they came from.

6
Bid Opening

We open the envelopes — in order

On closing day we close the tender, then open the technical envelopes and score the quality. Only afterwards do we open the financial envelopes and see the prices.

Why it matters: quality is judged without anyone knowing the prices. It's the single best protection against picking a favourite and justifying it afterwards.

In the system

On the Bid Opening step, in this order: Close Tender for Submissions → Open Technical Envelope → later Open Financial Envelope.

Try to open anything while the tender is still accepting bids and the system refuses, telling you the closing date. Whoever opened each envelope, and when, is recorded permanently.

Once opened, the bids flow into the list by themselves — nobody retypes them. Upload the opening minutes and attendance sheet against the same step.

7
Evaluation

We score them, and negotiate if we need to

The panel scores the offers against the criteria we agreed in step 3 and uploads the evaluation report. For consultancies there may be an interview stage. Then, if there's anything to settle on price or scope, we negotiate and write down what was agreed.

Why it matters: the decision and the reasoning are both on file, so anyone auditing us later can see not just who won, but why.

In the system

On Evaluation & Approvals, upload the evaluation report, score matrix or interview scores — the slots change depending on what you're buying.

Negotiations is optional. If you didn't hold any, click Skip Step and the case moves on. Otherwise upload the minutes.

Finish a stage with Complete Step, which notifies the requester and stakeholders that it's moved on.

8
Award

We award it and the contract is born

We pick the winner. The system checks one final time that they're still allowed to work with us, then turns the request number into a contract number and creates the contract. For goods it can also raise the purchase order.

Why it matters: the same number now follows the job from the original request all the way to the final payment.

In the system

On the Contract Award step, click Award Contract and choose the winner.

What happens next, without anyone doing it: the contract is created in Contract Management, the award is written onto the supplier's profile, and the original request is marked Converted to Sourcing.

For goods, Generate Purchase Order raises the PO — which routes to the COO for signature if it's $10,000 or more, otherwise to the Procurement Head.

And the job now begins

Sourcing ends the moment the contract exists. Everything after that — delivering, checking, invoicing, paying, closing — is the contract management flow.

Demonstrating this? The sealed-envelope part is the one people remember. Record a bid, then show it sitting in the sourcing case as Sealed — supplier and timestamp visible, contents not. Then try Open Technical Envelope before closing the tender and let the system refuse you.

The words people use

Sourcing case
One buying exercise, from approved request to signed contract.
Solicitation
The pack of documents telling suppliers what we want.
ITB / RFP
Invitation to Bid (for goods) / Request for Proposals (for services).
EoI
Expression of Interest — a short "yes, I'm interested and here's why I'm suitable".
Shortlist
The few we invite to make a full offer.
Bid / offer
What a supplier sends back.
Envelope
A sealed part of a bid. Technical and financial are opened separately.
Bid opening
The formal moment the sealed offers are unsealed.
Award
Choosing the winner.
Purchase order
The formal instruction to a supplier to deliver goods.
