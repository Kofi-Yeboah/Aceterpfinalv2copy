How ACET measures its work

Six flows, start to finish. Each is a short page saying what happens, why it matters, where it is done, and what changes when it does.

Where this is today

Everything described here is how the module is designed to work, and the forms are complete — the framework builder, the four-part indicator definition, the breakdown by group, the evidence upload and the rule that demands an explanation when performance falls short.

What is not finished is the plumbing underneath. Nothing entered on one screen yet reaches another, and entries are lost when you navigate away — so a framework you build will not appear in the indicator form, and a result you record will not move the dashboard. Treat each screen as a working demonstration of its own step rather than a chain you can walk end to end.

One control is worth adding early when this is built out: stop the person who entered a result from being the one who verifies it — the same rule procurement already applies to accepting deliverables.

The six flows

Each one is a short page. They follow the chain from framework to donor report.

Deciding what change we want

Flow 01

Writing down what we are trying to achieve and how our work is supposed to achieve it. That is a framework.

Deciding what to count

Flow 02

Turning a desired change into a specific, countable indicator — with a definition, a baseline and a target.

Collecting the numbers

Flow 03

Reporting what actually happened each period, broken down by group, with the evidence attached and an explanation where performance fell short.

Proving the numbers are true

Flow 04

Someone other than the person who entered a figure checks it against the evidence before it can reach a donor.

Reporting to donors

Flow 05

Tracking every reporting obligation, so nothing is missed and lateness is visible rather than quietly absorbed.

Reporting to ourselves

Flow 06

Management reporting, the M&E function’s own reporting, and formal impact evaluation.
