How ACET measures its work

Six flows, start to finish. Deciding what change we are trying to make, deciding what to count, collecting the numbers, proving they are true, and turning them into the reports donors and management ask for. Each step says what happens, why it matters, where it is done, and what changes when it does.

The one idea behind all of it

Every screen in this module is one link in a single chain. A framework says what change we are trying to cause. An indicator is the thing we will count to know whether it is happening. A record is that count for one period, with the evidence behind it. Checking confirms the evidence supports the number. A report assembles the checked numbers.

That chain is also the path a donor walks in reverse: from a figure in a report, back to the record, to the file that proves it, to why we were measuring that in the first place. Every link has to hold.

The measurement chain — the whole module in one line

Flow 01

Deciding what change we want

Before anything is measured, someone has to write down what we are trying to achieve and how our work is supposed to achieve it. That is a framework.

1
M&E lead

The framework is set up

A name, a type, what it covers, and the period it runs for. It is either institutional — about the whole organisation — or attached to a specific programme or project.

If you choose programme or project, a box appears asking which one. If you choose institutional, that box is not there at all — there is nothing to attach it to.

In the system

Monitoring & Evaluation › MEL Frameworks

Click Add New Framework. The signed framework document itself can be uploaded or linked.

2
M&E lead

The chain of results is built

The heart of the framework. Four levels by default, each one explaining the one above it:

Goal / ImpactThe big change — "reduce youth unemployment by 20%"
OutcomeWhat has to become true for that — "young people gain skills employers want", "employers actually hire them"
OutputWhat our work directly produces — "training hubs built", "certification launched"
ActivityWhat we actually do day to day — "renovate the buildings", "write the curriculum", "sign agreements with employers"

Every item names the item above it that it contributes to. Levels can be added, renamed or removed, so a framework that needs five tiers or different wording is not forced into this shape.

Why it matters This is what lets you answer "why are we doing this activity?" by tracing upward, and "what are we actually doing about this goal?" by tracing downward. An activity with nothing above it is work nobody can justify.
3
M&E lead

The whole thing is reviewed before it is saved

The builder ends with a review of the complete structure rather than saving it level by level, so it is confirmed as one coherent piece.

What changes once a framework exists
  • Indicators now have something to hang off — before this, there is nothing to attach them to.
  • Every result the organisation has committed to is written down in one place.
  • The framework can be pointed at when a donor asks how the work is supposed to produce the change.
Connects to

Project Management — a project-level framework belongs to a project. CRM › Grant Management — donors usually require a framework as part of the agreement.

Flow 02

Deciding what to count

A framework says what change we want. An indicator is the specific, countable thing that tells us whether it is happening.

Namedwhat it is, precisely defined
→
Classifiedwhose indicator, which framework
→
Measurement rulesunit, source, how often
→
Benchmarkswhere we started, where we're going

Four sections — an indicator is not properly defined until all four are done

1
M&E lead

Name it, and define it precisely

"Youth trained in digital skills" sounds obvious until you have to decide whether someone who attended two of five sessions counts. The definition field is where that gets settled, once, in writing.

In the system

Monitoring & Evaluation › Performance Indicators

Click Add New Indicator.

2
M&E lead

Say whose indicator it is, and where it is measured

Two separate questions, and they are easy to confuse. Whose indicator it is decides which report it rolls into. Where it is measured decides who collects it. They vary independently — see the two things called "level" below.

It also names the framework it belongs to, and the specific outcome inside that framework it reports against.

3
M&E lead

Write down exactly how it will be measured

The unit, where the data comes from, and how often it is collected. Alongside that sits a document called a reference sheet — the definitive statement of what counts, who collects it, from where, and how it is worked out.

Why it matters Programmes run for years and people move on. Without the reference sheet, the person collecting in year three counts something slightly different from the person who collected in year one, the trend line becomes meaningless, and nobody notices until a donor asks.
4
M&E lead

Record where we are starting and where we are going

The baseline is the value before the work started. The target is where it should get to, and by when.

Why it matters Without a baseline, "500 people trained" means nothing — you cannot tell whether that is a transformation or business as usual. The target tells you where you wanted to get to; the baseline tells you how far you have actually come.
What changes once an indicator exists
  • It appears in the register for everyone to see, with its target and its current status.
  • Results can now be collected against it each period.
  • It counts towards the indicator totals on the dashboard and in reports.
Flow 03

Collecting the numbers

Each period, someone reports what actually happened against each indicator — and has to show their working.

1
Field or programme staff

The actual figure is entered

For a given indicator and a given period — "Q1 2025 (Jan–Mar)" — what was achieved, against the target that was already set.

In the system

Monitoring & Evaluation › Data Collection

2
Field or programme staff

The figure is broken down, not just totalled

Alongside the headline number, the form asks for the split — men and women, urban and rural.

Why it matters A programme can hit its overall target of 500 people and have reached almost no women, or nobody outside the capital. The total hides that completely. Most donors require the breakdown, and it is far easier to capture as you go than to reconstruct a year later.
3
Field or programme staff

Evidence is attached

Attendance sheets, photographs, survey results, a report — whatever substantiates the number. The row shows how many pieces of evidence are attached.

Why it matters A number with no evidence behind it cannot survive an audit. This is the difference between reporting a result and being able to prove one.
4
The system

If performance fell short, an explanation is required

The system works out achievement as the actual against the target. If it comes out below 85 per cent, an alert appears and the record is not treated as complete until someone writes down why.

85% or aboverecord can be submitted as it is
|
Below 85%explanation required before it counts as complete
Why it matters This is the module's most important rule. An under-performing indicator with a reason attached is useful — the rains failed, the partner withdrew, the assumption was wrong. The same number with no explanation is just bad news that teaches nobody anything. Forcing the explanation at the moment of entry is what turns monitoring into learning.
What changes when a record is entered
  • The indicator's actual value moves, and its status may flip between On Track, At Risk and Behind.
  • The record joins the queue waiting to be checked.
  • Nothing reaches a donor report yet — it is not verified.
Flow 04

Proving the numbers are true

Somebody other than the person who entered it opens the record, looks at the evidence, and decides whether the number stands.

Draftbeing entered
→
Pendingwaiting to be checked
→
Verifiedevidence supports it — safe to report
or
Flaggedsomething is wrong — goes back

Only verified records should reach a donor report. That is the entire reason the status exists.

1
M&E reviewer

The check is against the evidence, not the number

The question is not "does this look plausible" but "does the attached evidence actually show this". A record claiming 550 people trained with an attendance sheet listing 300 gets flagged.

In the system

Monitoring & Evaluation › Data Collection

Filter to Pending and use the row menu to verify.

2
M&E reviewer

Flagged records go back to be fixed

Flagging is not a rejection of the work — it usually means the evidence needs to be better, or the number needs recounting. It returns for correction and comes back round.

Why it matters Every number that goes to a donor has had a second pair of eyes on it. When a figure is challenged months later, there is a named person who checked it and evidence sitting behind it.
What changes when a record is verified
  • It becomes usable in donor and M&E reporting.
  • It counts towards the data quality score on M&E reports.
  • The indicator's achievement is now a figure the organisation stands behind.
Flow 05

Reporting to donors

Every grant comes with reporting obligations. This is where they are tracked, so nothing is missed and lateness is visible.

1
M&E and Finance

Each obligation is a row

The donor, the project, what kind of report it is — quarterly financial, quarterly narrative, annual progress, mid-term evaluation, annual audit — the period it covers, when it is due, and when it actually went.

In the system

Monitoring & Evaluation › Reporting & Analytics › Donor Reports

2
The system

"Did we send it" and "was it on time" are two different columns

The status says whether it has gone: Submitted, In Draft, Due Soon or Overdue. The compliance column says whether it went on time: On Time, Late, Pending or Overdue.

Why it matters A report can be submitted and late — due 30 April, sent 2 May. Collapsing the two columns into one would hide exactly the thing donor relationships turn on. Being able to say "we have never missed a deadline" requires having tracked the deadline, not just the delivery.
What changes when a report is submitted
  • The obligation is closed for that period, and the compliance record is set.
  • The reporting compliance rate on the dashboard moves.
  • The numbers in it are now the organisation's official position with that donor.
Connects to

CRM › Grant Management — the agreement that created the obligation. Finance — financial reports need the spending figures. Data Collection — the results reported must be verified ones.

Flow 06

Reporting to ourselves

Three more kinds of reporting, aimed inward — at management, at the M&E function, and at the question of whether any of it actually worked.

Management reports

Internal reporting across the organisation — portfolio performance, budget burn rate, resource use, the risk register, procurement status, cash flow forecast, and the quarterly board report. Each has a frequency, a period, who produced it, whether it is draft or final, and which way the trend is going.

In the system

Monitoring & Evaluation › Reporting & Analytics › Management Reports

M&E reports

The M&E function's own reporting — performance against indicators, framework reviews, beneficiary reach, data quality assessments, learning briefs, gender and inclusion tracking. Two columns set these apart: a data quality score and how many indicators the report covers.

Why both numbers are needed A report covering 45 indicators at 92% quality and one covering 6 at 85% are very different things. One number alone cannot tell them apart, and treating them as equivalent flatters the weaker one.

Impact assessment

Formal evaluations asking whether the change actually happened and whether we caused it. Each has a method, a scope, a date range, how many people it covers, and — once analysis is done — an impact score.

MethodIn plain terms
Randomised trialSome people get the programme, some do not, chosen at random. The strongest evidence, and the hardest and most expensive to do.
Quasi-experimentalA comparison group put together rather than chosen at random — used where randomising would be impossible or unfair.
Before and afterMeasure the same group before and after. Simple, but cannot separate our effect from everything else happening at the same time.
Mixed methodsNumbers plus interviews and observation. Each covers the other's blind spot.
Contribution analysisTests whether the story in the framework held up, rather than trying to isolate our share. Honest where many organisations were involved.
Cost-benefitCompares what was achieved against what it cost. Answers "was it worth it", not "did it work".

An assessment still collecting data has no impact score — the column reads zero rather than showing an invented figure. That is deliberate: a score before analysis would be a guess presented as a finding.

The dashboard

Summary figures, gender split, funding by donor type, indicator achievement over time, grant utilisation, reporting compliance, and beneficiary reach — filterable by date, project and donor, with panels that can be switched off.

The pairing to watch Reporting compliance sits next to data quality. Compliance says reports went out on time; quality says whether the numbers in them had been checked. High compliance with low quality — filing punctually with unverified figures — is the failure mode worth catching, and it is invisible unless both are on the same screen.
Reference

Which framework type to use

All four describe how activities lead to change, and people use the names interchangeably. They answer different questions, and picking the wrong one produces a document nobody uses.

TypeAnswersReach for it when
Theory of Change Why do we believe this will work? Designing a programme, or defending the logic to a sceptical board. It is the only one that writes down the assumptions — the things that must hold true but that we do not control.
Results Framework What are we accountable for delivering? Reporting performance upward. Strips out the narrative and keeps the results with their indicators and targets. This is the performance one.
Logical Framework How will this be managed and checked? Running the work and satisfying a donor who requires a logframe. A grid: results down the side, and for each one its indicator, how it will be verified, and what is being assumed. This is the structural one.
Logic Model What goes in, what comes out? Explaining a project on one page to a general audience. The simplest, and the least useful for measurement.
Structure versus performance, in one line

A logical framework is structural — it fixes the design, how each result will be verified, and what is being assumed, and you use it to run the work.

A results framework is about performance — it drops the design detail and keeps the results with their indicators and targets, and you use it to report the work.

Many organisations keep both: the logframe for delivery, the results framework for the donor. If you do, the two must name the same results — that is where they usually drift apart.

The two things called "level"

The indicator table has two columns with "level" in the name. They are genuinely different, and this is the most common point of confusion in the module.

Indicator LevelProject Level
AnswersWhose indicator is this — who needs the number?Where in the project is it measured?
OptionsProject level (donor requirement) · Programme level · Institutional KPIProject · Phase · Deliverable · Task
DecidesWhich report it rolls up intoWhat it is attached to, and therefore who collects it

They move independently, which is why both columns exist. An institutional KPI — say, overall budget variance — can be measured at project level inside one specific project. A donor-required indicator might be measured at deliverable level, against the training curriculum that was actually handed over. Knowing one tells you nothing about the other.

In the table, Project Level shows the level as a tag with the thing it is attached to underneath — the phase, deliverable or task by name. At project level that second line is left out, because it would simply repeat the Project column next to it.

The pattern worth noticing

M&E sits across everything rather than at the end of a chain. Procurement buys what the activity needs, finance pays for it and tracks the cost, projects deliver it — and M&E is the only module asking whether any of it produced the change we said it would.

Reference

Words people use

Framework
The written statement of what change we are trying to cause and how our work causes it.
Indicator
The specific countable thing that tells us whether that change is happening.
Baseline
The value before the work started. Without it, an actual has nothing to be compared to.
Target
Where the indicator should get to, and by when.
Actual
What was really achieved in a period.
Achievement
The actual as a percentage of the target. Below 85%, an explanation is required.
Disaggregation
Splitting a figure by group — men and women, urban and rural — so the total cannot hide who was missed.
Evidence
The attendance sheet, photo, survey or report that proves the number. A figure without it cannot survive an audit.
Reference sheet
The definitive written statement of how an indicator is measured, so it means the same thing years later.
Verification
Someone other than the person who entered a figure confirming the evidence supports it.
Means of verification
Where the evidence for an indicator is supposed to come from. A required column in a logframe.
Assumption
Something that must be true for the plan to work but that we do not control — the rains, an election, a partner staying involved.
Output vs outcome
An output is what we produced — a training hub built. An outcome is what changed as a result — people gaining skills and jobs. We control outputs; we only influence outcomes.
Attribution vs contribution
Attribution says our programme caused the change. Contribution says we helped. Where several organisations were involved, contribution is the honest claim.
Outcome harvesting
Starting from changes you can see and working backwards to what caused them, instead of only checking the indicators you set in advance.
Data quality
How much of the reported data has been verified against evidence. Reported alongside compliance for a reason.
