How ACET manages a contract

What happens after we've chosen a supplier — from signing, to paying, to closing the job — in nine plain steps.

1 · Contract is created 2 · We fill in the details 3 · We watch the dates 4 · They deliver 5 · Someone checks it 6 · The bill arrives 7 · Four checks, then pay 8 · If things change 9 · Score and close
1
Contract Created

The contract comes into being

Once a winner has been chosen in Sourcing, the contract is created for us automatically. The request number becomes the contract number — so PR-2026-101 becomes CNT-2026-101. Occasionally we sign something outside that route, and then we type it in by hand.

Why it matters: keeping the same number means we can always walk backwards from a payment to the original request that authorised it.

In the system

Procurement › Contract Management

Contracts that came from Sourcing are already in the list. For anything else, click Register Contract to open a five-step form.

2
Setting It Up

We fill in the details

A contract needs more than a number. We record who is looking after it, what the supplier must hand over and by when, how much we pay at each stage, and we attach the signed document. We can name up to three coordinators — the people who get every alert about it.

Why it matters: everything later in this flow — deliverables, payments, reminders, close-out — is measured against what we write down here.

In the system

The five steps of the form are: 1 who and what · 2 money terms · 3 what they must deliver and when · 4 the payment schedule · 5 the signed contract and up to three coordinators.

Choose Lump Sum for a fixed price, or Time Based for a daily/weekly/monthly rate — which then asks for a maximum amount it can never exceed.

If a contract came over from Sourcing with nothing filled in, the screen shows Complete contract setup, which opens the same form.

3
Monitoring

We watch the dates

From now on the system watches the calendar so nobody has to remember. It chases the coordinator a week before something is due, and again when it's late. If an approval sits untouched for two days it sends a reminder; after three it tells someone more senior.

Why it matters: most contract problems are just things quietly going past their date. Nothing here depends on a person remembering.

In the system

Procurement › Contract Management

The top of the screen shows alert cards: contracts expiring, deliverables overdue, invoices waiting, variations pending.

Procurement › Reporting & Analytics › Contracts

The whole portfolio in one table — value, spend, expiry and risk — with the counts that matter on the cards above it.

4
Deliverables

The supplier delivers, and we file the proof

The supplier hands over the work. The contract coordinator uploads the evidence — the report, the delivery note, the inspection form, the photos. For goods and works they also tick that the items were received and passed inspection.

Why it matters: this is the evidence that later justifies paying the bill. No evidence, no payment.

In the system

Procurement › Contract Management › open the contract › Deliverables

Click Upload Deliverable, choose the milestone, attach the real files, and submit.

Whatever you choose, it saves as Submitted — never as accepted. Only somebody else can accept it, which is step 5.

5
Acceptance

A second person checks it

Someone other than the person who uploaded it opens the evidence and decides: accept it, put it under review, or reject it. Rejecting requires them to write down what's wrong, and it goes back to the coordinator to fix.

Why it matters: one person cannot both do the work and sign it off. This is the single most important control in the whole contract flow.

In the system

On the same Deliverables tab: Accept, Under Review or Reject. Everything waiting on a decision is also collected in one queue at Procurement › Approvals › Deliverable Acceptance.

If you try to accept something you uploaded yourself, the system stops you and says so plainly. Switch to another user with the name badge at the top-right.

6
Invoice Received

The bill arrives

The supplier sends their invoice by email or on paper, and the coordinator captures it against the contract.

Why it matters: however it arrives, it lands in the same queue and follows the same rules. There's no side door.

In the system

Procurement › Invoices › Add Invoice

Click Add Invoice. The document is uploaded first and the detail fields stay locked until it is — choosing the contract then fills in the supplier automatically.

The system refuses a duplicate invoice number, and refuses any invoice that would take the total above what the contract allows — it tells you to raise a variation first.

Opening an invoice afterwards shows the uploaded document itself, embedded in the page, so the approver reads the actual bill rather than a typed-in figure.

7
Invoice Approval

Four checks, then we pay

The bill passes through four different people, in order. Each one has to finish before the next can start.

1. CoordinatorMatches it to accepted work
2. ProcurementChecks it against the contract
3. SupervisorGives final approval
4. FinancePays and records it
Why it matters: money only leaves after four separate people have each confirmed their own part.

In the system

On the Invoices & Payments tab each invoice shows a stage strip, and only the button for the stage it's actually at: Match & Review → Procurement Review → Supervisor Approval → Process Payment.

At any stage, Query sends the bill back to the supplier with a written reason; Resubmit puts a corrected one back at the start.

Procurement › Invoices

The same queue across every contract, for whoever is working through a stack of them.

What the system will not let you do
  • Pay a bill that hasn't been through all four stages.
  • Approve a bill that isn't linked to a deliverable we already accepted.
  • Skip the "this meets the contract specification" confirmation.
  • Give supervisor approval if you were the one who did the coordinator review.
  • Record a payment without a bank reference number.
8
Change Management

If something needs to change

Sometimes the job grows, or runs late, or the scope shifts. That's allowed — but a change to a signed contract goes through the same approvals as a brand new purchase: department head, Procurement, Finance, then the COO. Once all four agree, the system rewrites the contract value and the end date by itself and files a numbered amendment.

Why it matters: contracts can't quietly grow. Every increase is approved by four people and permanently recorded as Amendment 1, 2, 3 and so on.

In the system

Procurement › Contract Management › open the contract › Change Management

Click New Change Request, tick what's changing (scope, time, cost, terms — you can tick more than one), write the reason, and attach the supporting document. The form won't submit without one.

As you type "2 weeks" or "3 months", the screen shows you the new end date and the new contract value before you send it.

Once sent, it moves through the four approvers in Procurement › Approvals › Contract Variations, which shows whose turn it is.

9
Performance & Close-Out

We score them, then close the job

At the end (and halfway through, for long jobs) we score the supplier out of 10 on quality, timeliness, cost control, following the rules, and professionalism. The coordinator scores; a supervisor approves. Then we run the closing checklist, print the certificate, and shut the contract.

Why it matters: a score below 5 flags the supplier automatically, and next time they need management permission before we can use them. The rating is what makes good behaviour worth something.

In the system

… open the contract › Performance

Pick Mid-Term or Final, slide the five scores, add a supervisor, submit. The score appears on the supplier's profile in Supplier Management on its own — you don't copy it across.

… open the contract › Close-Out

The checklist ticks itself where it can: all deliverables accepted, all invoices paid, final evaluation done, signed contract on file. Only "Procurement confirms compliance" is a manual tick. Generate the Certificate of Completion and Final Closure Report, then Close Contract.

If something is outstanding, the button refuses and tells you exactly what's missing — for example "2 invoices not yet paid: INV-DV-004…".

The job is done — and it feeds the next one

The closed contract stays on file with its full history, and the supplier's score follows them into the next tender. Good performance earns more work; poor performance needs management approval before they can bid again.

Demonstrating this? The invoice chain in step 7 is the best thing to show. Start as Ama (coordinator), switch to Felix (Procurement), then James (supervisor), then Abena (Finance) — using the name badge at the top-right. Try clicking the buttons out of order first; the refusals are the point.

The words people use

Contract coordinator
The person looking after the contract day to day. Up to three per contract.
Deliverable
A thing the supplier promised to hand over — a report, goods, finished work.
Milestone
A point in the contract where something is due.
Lump sum
One fixed price for the whole job.
Time based
Paid per day, week or month, up to a maximum.
Invoice
The bill from the supplier.
Query
Sending a bill back to be corrected.
Variation / amendment
An approved change to a signed contract.
Close-out
Finishing the contract properly and filing everything.
Audit trail
The permanent record of who did what, and when.
