How ACET manages suppliers

How a company or consultant gets onto our approved list, stays on it, and what happens if they let us down — in nine plain steps.

1 · They ask to join 2 · They send papers 3 · System screens them 4 · Procurement approves 5 · Finance checks the bank 6 · They can get work 7 · Papers stay current 8 · Every job is scored 9 · Slip up, get flagged
1
Registration

Someone asks to work with ACET

Procurement enters the supplier — their details, their contacts and the documents they gave us — usually after meeting them or receiving their company profile.

Why it matters: typing a name in does not make it usable. The same checks apply to everyone, so nobody gets on the list by knowing someone.

In the system

Procurement › Supplier Management › Onboard Firm

The button changes with the tab you're on — Onboard Firm or Onboard Individual Consultant. Fill in the details and finish with Complete Onboarding.

Approvals › Supplier Registrations

Everything entered waits here until Procurement approves it, so a new name cannot be used the moment it is typed in.

2
Documents

They send their papers

A company gives us its certificate of incorporation, tax clearance, SSNIT clearance, VAT registration, references and a signed compliance form. An individual consultant gives us a CV, a passport or national ID, proof of where they live, and bank confirmation.

Each document that expires is recorded with its expiry date.

Why it matters: the expiry date is the important bit — it's what lets the system chase them for a renewal later, instead of us finding out too late.

In the system

In the onboarding form there's a row per document. Attach the real file and type its expiry date next to it.

Renewed certificates are attached to the supplier's record on the Documents tab of their profile.

3
Automatic Screening

The system checks them, on its own

As soon as the name is typed, four checks run without anyone asking. Is this already on our list under a slightly different name? Is this name on a banned list? Are any documents missing or already expired? And, based on all of that, how risky are they?

Why it matters: these are the checks people forget or skip when they're busy. The system doesn't get busy.

In the system

Warnings appear as you type the legal name, before you've even finished the form. When you submit, a summary panel lists everything found before the record is saved.

The four automatic checks
  • Duplicate — ignores "Ltd", "Inc", "Ghana" and so on, so "Tech Solutions Inc." and "Tech Solutions" are spotted as the same firm.
  • Banned list — matches against donor and statutory debarment names. A hit raises an urgent alert and blocks approval.
  • Documents — flags anything missing, expired, or expiring within 30 days.
  • Risk level — Low, Medium or High, worked out from their score, their documents and their status. It's recalculated every time anything changes.
4
Approval

Procurement approves them

A new registration is not live. It sits waiting until someone in Procurement reads it, looks at the warnings, and approves it. Only then does it get a supplier ID number and join the approved list.

Why it matters: a supplier cannot be given work while they're still waiting. The system simply won't offer them in Sourcing.

In the system

Procurement › Supplier Management › Pending Review

Everything waiting sits in this tab. Open one and click Approve Registration.

If a mandatory document is still missing, the button refuses and names the missing documents rather than just failing.

5
Banking

Finance confirms the bank account

Separately from Procurement, someone in Finance checks that the bank account really belongs to this supplier, and marks it confirmed. Their name is recorded against it.

Why it matters: the people who choose a supplier are not the people who confirm where the money goes. That split is deliberate.

In the system

Procurement › Supplier Management › open the supplier › Overview

In the banking panel, click Validate Banking Details. Only a Finance user sees this button working; everyone else sees it greyed out with the reason.

Account numbers stay masked everywhere else. If the account is later changed, the confirmation clears itself and Finance has to check it again.

6
The Approved List

Now they can be given work

From here on they appear in Sourcing when we're picking who to invite. Every time anyone tries to invite or award them, the system re-checks them right at that moment — not once at registration, but every single time.

Why it matters: a supplier who was fine in March but whose tax certificate lapsed in July gets caught in July, not next year.

In the system

Nothing to do here — this happens by itself inside Sourcing. The supplier picker shows each supplier's rating, category and status, sorted best first.

Green — go aheadActive, papers current. Invite or award freely.
Amber — needs sign-offFlagged, scored under 5, or a lapsed document. Management must approve first.
Red — blockedSuspended, blacklisted, on a banned list, or still awaiting approval. Cannot be picked at all.
7
Keeping Current

Their papers have to stay current

Certificates expire. The system watches every expiry date and starts chasing before it happens — the supplier gets asked for a fresh copy, and Procurement is told too.

Why it matters: nobody has to keep a spreadsheet of expiry dates. And an expired certificate doesn't quietly slip through — it turns them amber at step 6.

In the system

Procurement › Supplier Management › Document Expiry

A live list of everyone with something expired, expiring, or never provided.

Procurement › Reporting & Analytics › Supplier Performance

The Document Issues card counts everyone with something expired or missing, and the table names them.

8
Performance

Every job they do gets scored

At the end of each contract, the coordinator scores the supplier out of 10 on quality, timeliness, cost control, following the rules, and professionalism. A supervisor approves the score. It then appears on the supplier's profile by itself — nobody copies it across.

Why it matters: their headline rating is the average of every job they've ever done for us, not just the last one or somebody's opinion.

In the system

Procurement › Supplier Management › open the supplier › Performance

Shows every evaluation with its contract number, who scored it, who approved it, and the score on each criterion.

Procurement › Supplier Management › Performance

The league table — everyone ranked by score, so you can see at a glance who's worth using again.

Scores are created in Contract Management › Performance at the end of a job, not typed in here.

9
Flag, Suspend, Blacklist

If they let us down

A score below 5 flags them automatically — no one has to decide. On top of that, a person can flag, suspend or blacklist a supplier for fraud, poor work or breaking the rules. Every one of those needs a written reason, and it's kept for ever.

A blocked supplier can ask to come back, but only Senior Management can let them.

Why it matters: this is what makes the score mean something. A bad job actually costs a supplier their next contract.

In the system

Procurement › Supplier Management › open the supplier

Buttons: Flag Supplier, Suspend, Blacklist. Each opens a box that won't submit without a reason.

To bring one back: Request Reactivation, then a Senior Management user clicks Approve Reactivation.

Procurement › Supplier Management › Blocked

Everyone currently flagged, suspended or blacklisted — with the reason and who did it.

What blocking actually does
  • They stop appearing in the Sourcing supplier picker.
  • They can't be invited to a tender or awarded a contract.
  • The reason and who set it are kept permanently on their record.
  • Only Senior Management can reverse it.

One list, checked every time

Everything above feeds one approved list, and Sourcing checks that list fresh at every invitation and every award. That's the whole point: the checks aren't done once at registration and then trusted for years.

Demonstrating this? Two things land well. Type "Global Trade Partners Ltd" into the onboarding form and watch the banned-list warning appear before you finish typing. Then open Kwame Construction Ltd — flagged, scored 4.7 — and try to invite them in Sourcing. The system makes you get management approval first.

The words people use

Supplier
A company or person we buy from.
Onboarding
Getting a new supplier registered and approved.
Supplier ID
Their number on our list, e.g. SUP-1001.
Prequalified
Approved, papers current — allowed to bid.
Debarment / banned list
Names donors or governments have barred from public work.
Risk level
Low, Medium or High — worked out by the system, not typed in.
Flagged
A warning on their record. They can still work, with permission.
Suspended
Temporarily stopped, usually pending an investigation.
Blacklisted
Permanently stopped.
