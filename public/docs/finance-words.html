Words people use

The terms that come up in every finance conversation, in plain language.

Entry
One recorded event — money moving, or a value changing. Always written with two matching halves.
Posting
Making an entry final and part of the official books. Before posting it is a proposal; after, it counts.
Reversal
Cancelling a posted entry by adding its exact opposite. The original stays visible.
Ledger
The complete record of everything posted.
Account
A named bucket things are recorded against — a bank account, a category of spending, an asset.
Chart of accounts
The full list of those buckets, and how they are organised.
Accrual
Recording something in the month it actually happened, even though the invoice has not arrived yet.
Depreciation
Spreading the cost of a long-lived item across the years it will be used.
Period
A month. Periods are opened, worked in, then closed.
Soft close / hard close
Soft close means the checklist is under way and the month can still be reopened. Hard close means locked for good.
Trial balance
A list of every account's total, used to check the books still balance before producing anything else.
Variance
The gap between what was budgeted and what was spent. Under five per cent is treated as on target.
Unrealised gain or loss
A change in what foreign money is worth because the exchange rate moved. Nothing was bought or sold — it is on paper until it is converted.
Control account
A single summary total in the books that stands for a long detailed list kept elsewhere.
Reconciliation
Confirming that summary total and that detailed list still agree.
Fund
A pot of donor money with its own balance, kept separate because it was given for a purpose.
Ageing
Grouping money owed by how long it has been outstanding. Old debts are less likely to be collected.
Audit trail
The permanent record of who changed what, and what it was before and after.
