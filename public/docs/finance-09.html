One source, four views of it

ReportAnswers
Trial balanceEvery account and its total, and the proof that the two sides of the books still agree. The check before anything else is produced.
Income statementWhat came in, what went out, and the difference — the result for the period.
Balance sheetWhat the organisation owns, what it owes, and what is left over, at a point in time.
Closing balancesEach account before closing, the closing adjustment, and the figure it ends on. This is step 10 of the close made visible.
Project reportsMoney by project — what was budgeted against what was actually spent.
Audit trayThings an auditor has queried, held in one place waiting for a response.
Audit trailWho did what, when, and what the value was before and after they did it.
Timesheet reportsTime booked to projects — the link between what staff cost and what projects cost.
Why the audit trail is worth more than it looks It records the value before and after every change, not just that something changed. "Someone edited this" is not evidence. "This was 5,000, and Ama changed it to 8,000 on 14 March" is.

The dashboard

The finance dashboard collects the numbers people ask for most — income against spending, cash flow, budget utilisation, where funding comes from, who owes us and how long they have owed it, who we owe, payroll by department, bank balances and anything waiting for a decision. Panels can be switched off, so each person can keep the ones they use.

Money owed is grouped by how overdue it is — current, one to two months, two to three, three to four, and beyond four. The older a debt gets, the less likely it is ever to be paid, which is why age is shown rather than just a total.
