End-to-end procurement process flows

Sixteen flows, from the annual plan through to contract close-out and supplier rating. Each one names the actor at every station, the control that must be satisfied before it can advance, and where that control is enforced in the system. Read it as the operational manual and as the completeness check: no flow below has a dead end.

The golden thread

Plan→ Requisition→ Sourcing→ Award→ Contract→ Deliverables→ Invoice→ Payment→ Close-out→ Supplier rating

Every record carries the reference of the record above it, and the supplier rating produced at close-out feeds back into who may be shortlisted next time. Any payment can be traced back to the plan line that authorised it, and any award can be traced forward to how the supplier actually performed.

00

Cross-cutting foundations

Four services underpin every flow. They are what turn a set of screens into a controlled process.

Identity and permissions

Nine roles, each holding a set of capabilities. Controls enable themselves against the acting user's capabilities and explain why they are locked when they are not.

Two separation-of-duty rules are enforced in the data layer, not the screens, so no view can bypass them: a coordinator cannot accept a deliverable they submitted, and the supervisor approving an invoice cannot be the person who reviewed it.

lib/currentUser.ts

Notifications, reminders, escalation

Every stage transition dispatches a notification recording its channels, recipient and priority. Where a stage waits on somebody, a reminder is scheduled beside it and escalates if unanswered.

Completing the awaited action stops the chase. The sweep runs on load and each minute after.

lib/notificationStore.ts

Value thresholds

Four canonical methods with the value bands that suggest them. The system suggests; Procurement may override, but a deviation must carry a written justification.

Legacy method names are folded onto the four official methods so Planning, Requisitions and Sourcing all speak one language.

lib/procurementThresholds.ts

Audit trail

Contracts carry a full action log; requisitions carry an approval history; plan items carry field-level change history with old and new values; suppliers carry status history.

Every entry records the real acting user, the date and a reason.

01

Procurement planning

BR001

Nothing may be procured that is not on an approved plan. This flow is what puts it there, and the two review stations are what stop an unfunded or non-compliant line becoming usable. Plans are of two kinds — departmental, the annual plan each department prepares, and project, belonging to an approved project. They carry the same lifecycle but are reviewed against different budgets, so each has its own queue and neither can be approved on the other's.

Two-station compliance review before a plan line becomes usable

Steps

Controls

  • An item only appears in the requisition plan-item picker once it is approved by both stations.
  • The two plan types never mix. Each approval queue is filtered to its own plan type in the data layer, so a departmental entry cannot be approved on the project queue or the reverse.
  • Amendments to an approved item do not apply immediately. They are held pending until Procurement and Finance both approve, then written with full version history and both approvers stamped on the change record.
  • Overdue detection is automatic: completion dates are compared to today on load, slipped items flip to Delayed and raise an alert. It does not depend on anyone setting a status by hand.

Purchase requisition

BR001

The requisition is where the internal control lives: a validation gate before the workflow starts, five approval stations with two running in parallel, and a route back for anything rejected. 

flowchart TD

    B -- Fails --> A
    B -- Passes --> C["PENDING DEPT APPROVAL"]
    C -- "Reject with reason" --> R["REJECTED"]
    C -- Approve --> D["PENDING PROCUREMENT AND FINANCE
reviewed in parallel"]

    E -- Reject --> R
    F -- Reject --> R

    F --> G

Five stations, parallel procurement and finance review, and a resubmission route

The validation gate — submission is blocked until every one holds

  • Title, description, department, estimated cost, funding source and category are all present.
  • Goods carry a delivery timeline. Services, works and consultancy carry start and end dates, in the right order.
  • The requisition is linked to an approved plan activity, or carries an approved emergency override with a written justification.
  • The chosen method matches its value band, or a deviation justification is recorded.
  • Direct selection carries a justification and the supplier's full legal name, registered address and email. Limited competition carries its shortlist.
  • At least one mandatory document is attached — terms of reference for services, technical specifications for goods.
  • The funding source matches the linked plan item's funding source.

Lifecycle states

Draft Pending Dept Approval Pending Procurement and Finance Pending Senior Mgmt Approved Converted to Sourcing Rejected Withdrawn

Controls

  • Parallel approval. Procurement and Finance review concurrently; the requisition advances only when both have approved.
  • Threshold routing. Senior Management is inserted or skipped based on the $10,000 threshold.
  • Rejection is never silent. Every reject refuses an empty reason and returns an error.
  • Rejection is not terminal. Resubmission re-runs the full validation gate, returns the requisition to department approval and keeps a record of what was rejected, why, and how many attempts have been made.
  • Plan variance. A requisition costing more than 10% above its plan item is flagged, the requester must comment, and the flag is surfaced to every approver.
  • Days in stage is live, derived from when the requisition entered its current station.

Registration, screening, and the status transitions that gate future engagement

Automatic screening

  • Duplicate detection normalises away suffixes such as Ltd, Inc and GH before matching against the register.
  • Sanctions screening checks the debarment list; a hit raises an urgent notification and blocks approval.
  • Document expiry surfaces expired, expiring-within-30-days and missing documents separately.
  • Risk scoring is recomputed on every change from performance, document compliance, status and sanctions.
  • Renewal reminders are queued for every critical document within 60 days of expiry.

The eligibility gate

This is the single function Sourcing consults before any invitation or award.

ConditionResult

Flagged, performance below 5 out of 10, or expired compliance documents Conditional Permitted only with Senior Management approval
Missing documents, unvalidated banking, documents expiring soon Permitted Warning shown to the buyer

  • No supplier blocked by the eligibility gate can be invited or awarded.
  • A financial envelope cannot be opened before the tender closes, so technical scoring can be settled before prices are revealed.
  • Every stage transition notifies the requester and the internal stakeholders.

Bid submission and the sealed register

BR003 Sourcing

How offers get onto the record. Each offer is logged against the tender as it arrives, sealed on arrival, and cannot be read by anyone at ACET until the official opening after the tender closes. 

flowchart TD
    A["Offer arrives from supplier"] --> B["Procurement logs it on the Bid Opening step"]
    B --> C["Supplier, amount, date and documents captured"]
    C --> D["System issues submission ID
and acknowledgement reference"]
    D --> E["SEALED — timestamp recorded"]
    E --> F{"Tender still open?"}
    F -- Yes --> G["Entry may be withdrawn and replaced"]
    F -- No --> H["LOCKED"]
    H --> I["Procurement opens technical envelope"]
    I --> J["Technical evaluation"]
    J --> K["Procurement opens financial envelope"]
    K --> L["Financial evaluation"]

Sealed submission with staged envelope opening

Guarantees

  • Unique identity. Every submission receives a submission ID and an acknowledgement reference.
  • Sealed until opening. Submissions are locked on arrival; only the official opening unseals them, and only after the tender closes.
  • Audit trail. Timestamp, who logged it, document count and the name of whoever opened it are all recorded. The timestamps report exports to Excel or PDF.
  • Restricted tenders. A supplier not on the invitation list cannot be logged against a limited or direct tender.
  • Eligibility. Only active suppliers may be recorded; suspended and blacklisted ones are refused with an explanation.
  • No duplicate envelopes. One technical and one financial envelope per supplier per tender. Replacing requires withdrawing first, and only while the tender is open.
  • Consultant rates. Individuals may have a proposed daily or monthly rate captured where the tender requests it; the accepted rate is written to their rate history on award.

Everything else that touches suppliers is handled internally as well: registration and compliance documents in Supplier Management, registration approval in Approvals › Supplier Registrations, and invoices captured against a contract in Procurement › Invoices.

Contract registration and setup

BR004

A five-step wizard turns an award into a manageable contract.

StepCaptures
1 · IdentityTitle, auto-derived contract number, supplier, category, method, department, owner, funding source and budget line.
2 · Commercial termsLump sum or time based. Time-based contracts carry a rate frequency — daily, weekly, monthly or quarterly — and a maximum amount ceiling.
3 · ScheduleDelivery schedule for goods, with item, quantity and expected date; or milestones and deliverables for services.
4 · PaymentsA payment schedule tied to deliverables or timelines, validated against the contract value.
5 · Documents and coordinatorsThe signed contract or purchase order uploaded for real, and up to three contract coordinators assigned — hard-capped — who receive all contract notifications.

Registering seeds one pending deliverable per milestone and schedules the deliverable and expiry reminders.

Contracts arriving from a sourcing award

These are created as a shell carrying supplier, value and dates. The module detects the gap and offers to complete the contract setup, opening the same wizard in edit mode — so an awarded contract can always be brought up to a manageable state rather than sitting empty and unusable.

Coordinator, Procurement, Supervisor, Finance — with a query route at every stage

Gates

GateRule
On receiptInvoice number required and unique per contract. Amount above zero. Total invoiced may not exceed the contract ceiling, or the revised ceiling after an approved variation — otherwise a cost variation must be raised first.

Change management

BR004

A variation follows the same approval chain as a fresh procurement, in strict order, and is applied to the contract automatically once the chain completes. 

flowchart TD
    A["Coordinator or requestor raises change request"] --> B["Types: scope, time, cost,
terms or deliverables — multi-select"]
    B --> C["Mandatory: reason, description,
supporting documents, cost and time impact"]
    C --> D["System computes revised value
and revised completion date"]
    D --> E["Contract status becomes UNDER VARIATION"]
    E --> F["Department Head"]
    F --> G["Procurement"]
    G --> H["Finance"]
    H --> I["Senior Management"]
    I --> J["APPROVED"]
    F -- "Reject with reason" --> R["REJECTED"]
    G -- "Reject with reason" --> R
    H -- "Reject with reason" --> R
    I -- "Reject with reason" --> R
    J --> K["Applied automatically"]
    K --> L["Contract value revised"]
    K --> M["End date revised"]

Four sequential approvals, then automatic application to the contract

  • Amendments are numbered sequentially, 1, 2, 3 and so on.
  • Supporting documents are mandatory — a change request without them is rejected outright.
  • Time impacts expressed as "3 months", "2 weeks" or "14 days" are parsed into a real revised completion date, shown live as the user types.
  • Applying the change writes the revised value, end date and ceiling into the contract, plus a formal entry in the amendment register recording old and new values.
  • The contract returns to active unless another variation is still pending. Finance is notified so the budget and payment schedule can be revised.

Performance evaluation

BR002 BR004

Conducted at mid-term for long-duration and time-based contracts, and at final completion for every contract. Five default criteria on a ten-point scale — quality of deliverables, timeliness, cost control, compliance with terms, professionalism and responsiveness — which can be renamed, added to or removed. 

flowchart TD
    A["Contract Coordinator evaluates
five criteria on a ten-point scale"] --> B["Supervisor approves
must differ from the evaluator"]
    B --> C["Scorecard written to supplier profile"]
    C --> D["Headline score recomputed
as mean across all evaluations"]
    C --> E["Risk level recomputed"]
    D --> F{"Score below 5?"}
    F -- Yes --> G["Supplier automatically FLAGGED"]
    F -- No --> H["Supplier remains eligible"]
    G --> I["Future shortlisting or award
needs Senior Mgmt approval"]

The loop that makes a rating consequential rather than decorative

Two approvals are required, and an evaluation where the supervisor is the evaluator is refused. On submission the scorecard is pushed into the supplier profile, where it appears in both contract management and supplier management, and the headline score is recomputed as the mean across all evaluations — normalised to ten points so a re-weighted template still averages correctly.

Any supplier scoring below five is flagged automatically, after which engagement requires Senior Management approval through the eligibility gate in flow 03. That closing of the loop — a poor score on one contract actually blocking the next award — is the point of the exercise.

Reporting and analytics

BR007

Five reports, all computed from live data, all searchable, and all exportable to Excel, PDF and CSV. Each is the same shape: four summary cards across the top and one table beneath them.

ReportWhat it shows
Procurement PlanEvery plan activity with its approval status, planned value, method and target date. Cards: activities and how many are approved, planned value, awaiting review, delayed.
Purchase RequisitionsEvery requisition with its stage, value, department and days in stage. Cards: requisitions, awaiting approval, approved value, converted to sourcing.
ContractsThe portfolio — supplier, value, spend to date, end date and derived risk. Cards: active contracts, contract value, invoiced to date, expiring within 90 days.
Invoices & PaymentsEvery invoice with its contract, supplier, amount and position in the approval chain. Cards: invoices and total value, awaiting approval, paid, outstanding.

Contract risk is derived rather than declared. Overdue deliverables, repeated invoice queries, excessive variations, scope creep, cost growth above 20%, approaching expiry and poor performance scores each contribute, producing a low, medium or high severity.

The Approvals menu

  • Eight queues, in flow order: Purchase Requisitions · Sourcing · Departmental Plan Approval · Project Plan Approval · Contract Variations · Deliverable Acceptance · Supplier Registrations · Senior Management Approval.
  • Two stations sit elsewhere on purpose. A requisition's departmental approval belongs to the requester's own department head, and its budget check to Finance, so each appears in the menu of the person who has to act.
  • Invoice approvals have no separate queue. All four stations are worked on Procurement → Invoices, where each invoice shows a stage strip and only the button for the stage it is at.
