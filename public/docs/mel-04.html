Proving the numbers are true

Somebody other than the person who entered it opens the record, looks at the evidence, and decides whether the number stands.

Only verified records should reach a donor report. That is the entire reason the status exists.

1
M&E reviewer

The check is against the evidence, not the number

The question is not "does this look plausible" but "does the attached evidence actually show this". A record claiming 550 people trained with an attendance sheet listing 300 gets flagged.

In the system

Monitoring & Evaluation › Data Collection

Filter to Pending and use the row menu to verify.

2
M&E reviewer

Flagged records go back to be fixed

Flagging is not a rejection of the work — it usually means the evidence needs to be better, or the number needs recounting. It returns for correction and comes back round.

Why it matters Every number that goes to a donor has had a second pair of eyes on it. When a figure is challenged months later, there is a named person who checked it and evidence sitting behind it.
What changes when a record is verified
  • It becomes usable in donor and M&E reporting.
  • It counts towards the data quality score on M&E reports.
  • The indicator's achievement is now a figure the organisation stands behind.
